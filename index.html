The gap

You know the principles. Knowing hasn't moved anything.

You've read the books. Maybe twice. You can say the ideas out loud: write down what you want, hold the picture, stop competing, watch your words.

And the days still look the same, because a book gives you the idea once and then goes back on the shelf.

The authors saw this coming. Every one of them prescribed a practice. Read this aloud, morning and night. Hold this picture. Speak this word. The practice is the part that got lost between the page and your day.

Another year of knowing is the expensive option.

"I am not your teacher. I am a fellow student. What's in this box worked on me first."

I found these authors just before midlife. My back was against the wall, I had a family to provide for, and nothing under any of it. I had to sort out time, effort, money and livelihood all at once.

I applied what I found. That made all the difference.

Early on, an idea came to me: put the material in your hands as a tool you can use daily, the way the authors meant it to be used. So I built it.

How it works

Three steps to start. Four moves to use it.

  1. Pick the deck that fits

    Each book answers a different problem. The next section tells you which is yours.

  2. Read the manual, then break the seal

    The cards come sealed. The 16-page manual comes first: who wrote it, what the practice is, when to run it.

  3. Run the four moves

    Pull your Anchor, set your rhythm, and start the same day. The deck does the structuring.

Why trust it

The real thing, made daily.

Verbatim

Every card line is the author's own words. Each one is checked against the source text before it is printed. We name the cards and the states. We never rewrite the author.

Sourced

Every card names its chapter. Hill, 1937. Wattles, 1910. Shinn, 1925. All three texts are in the public domain, which is why you get the original and not our version of it.

Used first

The decks exist because the practices worked on the person who built them. The manual in each box opens with that story, in his words.

Every card traces back to a specific line, in a specific chapter, verified against the original text before it was printed. Not our interpretation. Their words, made daily.

Is this for you

Built for people who already believe the books.

This is for you if

  • You've read at least one of these books and the ideas haven't changed your days yet.
  • You want a tool you pick up and use, not another thing to read.
  • You're fine with the authors' own language, including God, Spirit and Infinite Intelligence.
  • You'd rather practice for ten minutes than study for two hours.

This isn't for you if

  • You want a summary or a modern paraphrase of the book.
  • You want a promise of results. The authors made their claims. The deck gives you the practice to test them.
  • You want an app, a streak counter or a course.

Questions

Before you decide

Is this a summary of the book?

No. Every line on a card is quoted from the original text, in the author's words, and checked against it. You still want the book. The deck is how you use it.

Which deck should I start with?

The one whose problem you recognized above. If two fit, start with Think and Grow Rich. It has the clearest daily rhythm: read your statement aloud, morning and night.

Do I need to share the authors' beliefs?

You don't have to, but the cards will use their language. These authors write about God, Spirit and Infinite Intelligence, and the decks keep every word as written. We don't soften it and we don't add to it.

Are the decks affiliated with the authors or their estates?

No. The texts are in the public domain. Power Decks are not affiliated with or endorsed by the Napoleon Hill Foundation or any other publisher or estate connected to these authors.
